Featured Supply Chain Glossary

Use this page as a quick reference. Each definition is intentionally short; the linked notes provide the longer context and examples.

Purchasing and procurement

Purchasing

The tactical, transaction-focused work of placing orders, tracking lead times, receiving goods, and managing routine buying activity.

Procurement

The broader discipline of acquiring goods and services, including sourcing strategy, supplier selection, negotiation, contracts, and performance.

Strategic sourcing

A structured approach to finding and developing suppliers while balancing total cost, quality, risk, lead time, and long-term value.

Purchase order

A formal buyer document that authorizes a supplier to provide specified goods or services under agreed terms.

Inventory and materials

Direct materials

Materials that become an identifiable part of the finished product, such as the glass in a phone screen or ingredients in packaged food.

MRO supplies

Maintenance, repair, and operating supplies that support the business but do not become part of the finished product.

Safety stock

Extra inventory held to protect against demand variation, supply disruption, forecast error, or uncertain lead time.

Holding cost

The cost of carrying inventory, including storage, capital, insurance, obsolescence, handling, and capacity impacts.

Planning and systems

ERP

Enterprise resource planning software that connects business functions and provides shared operational data.

MRP

Material requirements planning that calculates what materials are needed, in what quantity, and when they should be ordered.

Net requirements

The remaining demand after considering available inventory, scheduled receipts, safety stock, and other supply.

Projected available balance

The expected inventory position for a future period after subtracting demand and adding planned or scheduled supply.

Supplier relationships

SRM

Supplier relationship management: a cross-functional process for managing supplier performance, collaboration, contracts, risk, and long-term value.

Approved vendor list

A controlled list of suppliers evaluated and qualified to provide specific goods or services.

Total cost of ownership

The full lifecycle cost of a purchase or supplier relationship, beyond the initial price.

Planning, inventory, and operations

ABC analysis

A method of ranking inventory by annual usage value so that control effort matches business importance.

Bill of materials

A structured list of the components, quantities, and levels required to make a product.

Capacity planning

The process of comparing expected workload with available labor, equipment, supplier, and facility capacity.

Cycle stock

Inventory held to satisfy expected demand between replenishment deliveries.

Demand planning

The process of estimating future customer or operational demand to support supply, inventory, and capacity decisions.

Economic order quantity

A calculated order quantity that balances ordering cost and inventory holding cost under stated assumptions.

Expediting

Actions taken to accelerate a purchase, production order, shipment, or receipt when timing is at risk.

Forecast error

The difference between forecast demand and actual demand for a period.

Lead time

The elapsed time between starting a process and receiving its completed output.

Lot size

The quantity produced, purchased, or replenished in one order or run.

Reorder point

The inventory position at which a replenishment order should be triggered.

Service level

The likelihood or percentage of demand fulfilled without a stockout during a defined period.

Supply planning

The process of determining how inventory, production, purchasing, and capacity will satisfy expected demand.

Vendor-managed inventory

A replenishment arrangement in which the supplier monitors and manages inventory levels for the customer.

Procurement and supplier management

Blanket purchase order

An agreement authorizing repeated purchases from a supplier over a defined period and under defined terms. Also called a blanket order.

Incoterms

Standardized international trade terms that clarify responsibilities, costs, and risk for delivery.

Spend analysis

The structured review of purchasing data to identify suppliers, categories, demand patterns, savings, and risk.

Supplier scorecard

A recurring evaluation of supplier performance across measures such as quality, delivery, cost, service, and responsiveness.

Three-way match

A control that compares the purchase order, receipt record, and supplier invoice before payment.

RFQ

A request for quotation sent to suppliers to obtain comparable pricing and commercial terms.

Single sourcing

Using one supplier for a category, item, or requirement, often to gain coordination or volume benefits.

Multiple sourcing

Using two or more suppliers to improve resilience, competition, capacity, or geographic coverage.

Category management

Managing a group of related purchases as a strategic category rather than as isolated transactions.

Contract compliance

The degree to which purchases follow negotiated supplier terms, approved pricing, specifications, and processes.

Purchase requisition

An internal request for goods or services that starts the approval and purchasing process.

Supplier development

Structured work to improve a supplier’s capability, quality, delivery, cost, or resilience.

Supply disruption

An event that interrupts the expected flow of materials, services, information, or finished goods.

Working capital

Cash tied up in operating assets and liabilities, including inventory and accounts payable.

Inventory accuracy

The degree to which recorded inventory quantities and locations match the physical stock.

Supplier segmentation

Grouping suppliers by business impact, risk, spend, complexity, or relationship requirements.

Supplier risk

The possibility that a supplier will fail to meet requirements because of financial, operational, geographic, quality, compliance, or capacity issues.

Cost and performance

Avoided cost

A cost increase or expense that is prevented through a decision, negotiation, redesign, or process improvement.

Landed cost

The total cost of getting an item to its destination, including purchase price, freight, duties, insurance, and related charges.

Perfect order

An order delivered complete, on time, damage-free, accurately documented, and correctly invoiced.

Worked examples

Holding cost

If inventory is worth $20,000 and the annual carrying-cost rate is 25%, the estimated annual holding cost is:

$20,000 × 0.25 = $5,000

The rate may include capital, storage, insurance, handling, shrinkage, and obsolescence.

Reorder point

A simple reorder point can be estimated as:

average demand during lead time + safety stock

For example, 40 units of weekly demand, a two-week lead time, and 30 units of safety stock produce a reorder point of 110 units.
